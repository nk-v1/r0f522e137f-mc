The pipeline, in plain words

1
Brief

Two links with roles: source = script + voice-over, reference = the look only. End with "explain first, wait for my OK".

2
Listen

Whisper (speech-to-text) writes every line with start and end times. Those times drive every scene.

3
Study the look

The reference is cut into stills. Colours sampled, line widths measured, timing counted → a written style guide.

4
Approve the plan A

It explains the story beats, the style and the scene plan. You correct it, then say go.

5
Brushes

One shared toolkit: palette, outlines, clouds, sparkles, rays, pops, type, the product.

6
Scenes, in parallel

One small program per spoken beat. Helper agents write them at the same time, one scene file each.

7
Render + assemble B

12 pictures a second as PNG frames; ffmpeg joins them under the original voice-over into an MP4.

8
Critique → fix → verify

Helpers compare stills to the reference, fix, re-check. A judge scores it. (v2: 6.5/10, a to-do list, not a verdict.)

9
Your notes

Annotated screenshots: where · what's wrong · what instead. Plus a folder of real photos; it cuts out their backgrounds.

10
Version + compare C

Saved as vN, never overwritten. Side-by-side: older left, newer right.

11
Hand off D

HANDOFF.md + CLAUDE.md so a new session continues without re-explaining. Ask it to summarise and wait.

12
Export (optional)

After Effects: layers + keyframes (XML) + a build script. v4: 809 layers, 12,222 keyframes. Not yet tested inside AE.

Say these out loud

  • "Explain what you understood first. Wait for my OK."
  • "Play the MP4 with audio, not the HTML."
  • "Did you critique it against the reference?"
  • "Write my notes into a FEEDBACK file first."
  • "What did you just do?"

  • ae_export/ After Effects package
Why code, not a timeline? The AI can write code but can't drag keyframes. Every change is exact, and all 582 frames re-render in about 3 seconds. Reuse it: the reference-style-reel skill, prompts 01–09.
